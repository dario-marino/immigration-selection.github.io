Select a mode above, set a threshold, then click any country on the map to see exactly which sex × education × age-at-arrival groups from that country clear the bar (or, for the GDP and PISA modes, the country-level figure itself).
Sex × education × age-at-arrival groups computed from IPUMS USA / American Community Survey microdata (person-weighted). Income is total personal income minus Social Security, public-assistance, and Supplemental Security income (IPUMS INCTOT net of INCSS, INCWELFR, INCSUPP), adjusted for inflation. Institutionalization is the share of each group's ACS group-quarters population recorded in group quarters, per 100,000, restricted to ages 60 and under to reduce the elderly-care/nursing-home skew — ACS does not release which type of institution someone is in (correctional, mental health, and other institutional care are bundled together on the public file), so this is a proxy for institutionalization broadly, not a crime rate specifically. English proficiency is the share of each group reporting they speak English "very well" or that English is their only language. Groups built from fewer than 30 sampled records are marked unreliable and never count toward a country's qualifying total. GDP per capita is PPP, most recent year available. PISA scores are each country's average of the math, reading, and science mean scores from its most recent testing cycle (2022 for most participants); OECD does not publish an official single combined score, so this average is an editorial convention. A handful of PISA participants are non-sovereign or partial entities shown separately from a broader country (Hong Kong, Macao, Chinese Taipei); mainland China has no standalone national PISA score in this dataset, and several large countries of origin (including India) do not currently participate in PISA at all — these show as no-data on the PISA map regardless of threshold, not as zero. For the GDP and PISA modes, countries below a set threshold are shown in the excluded color regardless of group counts.
